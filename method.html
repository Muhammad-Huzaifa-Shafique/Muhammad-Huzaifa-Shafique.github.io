Checking instead of trusting

The same habit runs through the other projects: measure against something before claiming it works.

How I use AI assistants

My AI course required transparency about AI use, so I wrote it down. This is the short version of my own statement in the CyberAI report.

  • Writing code. I used ChatGPT like a pair-programming partner. I pasted Python errors, mostly from asynchronous and network code, and asked for suggestions. It helped me read obscure stack traces, suggested error-handling patterns and proposed refactors. I tested what it suggested instead of copying it blindly.
  • Learning. I asked for plain-language explanations of ideas such as retrieval-augmented generation. Those were a starting point. I then read papers and documentation to confirm them.
  • Writing reports. I gave it an outline, my module descriptions and the assignment instructions, and treated the output as a draft. I rewrote it, added my own examples and checked facts against my own code.
  • References. I used it to format APA references and cross-checked them against the original articles.
  • Disclosure. The first lines of my extractor scripts say AI was used and link the conversation, and my course reports carry an AI-use statement.

In my report I put it this way: "The responsibility for the final content is mine."

What I do not claim

  • I have not compared prompt wordings against a scored test set, so I do not claim these prompts are the best ones. The repositories hold no such comparison.
  • I have not measured Casefile's answer quality end to end. The retrieval comparison and the citation checker are the evidence so far.
  • I have not measured how accurate CyberAI is.
  • The citation checker proves that citations exist and quoted tokens match. It does not prove a sentence is true.
